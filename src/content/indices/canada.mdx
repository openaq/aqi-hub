## Background

Canada's Air Quality Health Index (AQHI) communicates the short-term health risk associated with exposure to a mixture of common air pollutants. The classic AQHI uses fine particulate matter (PM<sub>2.5</sub>), ground-level ozone (O<sub>3</sub>) and nitrogen dioxide (NO<sub>2</sub>). Canada began using the AQHI on 24 June 2007, replacing the previous Air Quality Index (AQI). Québec is the only province that uses the Info-Smog program instead of the standard AQHI. [^3][^4]

The AQHI is reported on a scale from 1 to 10+, with four health-risk categories. The federal forecast page describes forecast maximums for the next two days and nights, covering 72 hours. [^3]

## Color scale

The Government of Canada describes an 11-step scale: three blue levels for Low risk, three yellow/orange levels for Moderate risk, four pink/red levels for High risk, and a dark burgundy/brown level for Very High risk. [^3]

## Methods

### National AQHI

The classic Canadian AQHI is calculated from 3-hour rolling average concentrations of PM<sub>2.5</sub>, O<sub>3</sub> and NO<sub>2</sub>. The formula was developed using short-term associations between air pollution and health outcomes. [^5][^7]

$$
AQHI_{\text{classic}} =
\frac{10}{10.4} \times 100 \times
\left[
\left(e^{0.000871 \times NO_{2}} - 1\right)
+
\left(e^{0.000537 \times O_{3}} - 1\right)
+
\left(e^{0.000487 \times PM_{2.5}} - 1\right)
\right]
$$

Where:

- \(NO_{2}\) is the 3-hour rolling average nitrogen dioxide concentration in parts per billion (ppb).
- \(O_{3}\) is the 3-hour rolling average ground-level ozone concentration in ppb.
- \(PM_{2.5}\) is the 3-hour rolling average fine particulate matter concentration in micrograms per cubic metre (µg/m³).

The index represents the combined health risk associated with the pollutant mixture. The standard risk categories are Low (1–3), Moderate (4–6), High (7–10) and Very High (above 10). [^3][^5]

### Wildfire-smoke adjustment in the federal AQHI

The Government of Canada explains that, during a wildfire-smoke event, it adjusts the AQHI calculation to use hourly PM<sub>2.5</sub> alone. This is intended to respond more quickly to rapid increases in fine particulate matter during smoke events. The federal page describes this as an adjustment made during wildfire-smoke events; it does not provide the detailed calculation formula on that page. [^3]

## British Columbia: AQHI-Plus

### Background

British Columbia (B.C.) reports a modified AQHI that compares the classic three-pollutant AQHI with a separate index calculated from hourly PM<sub>2.5</sub>. The higher of the two values is reported. This approach is commonly referred to as AQHI-Plus. [^1][^5]

The reason for the modification is that wildfire smoke can cause rapid and substantial increases in PM<sub>2.5</sub>. In some B.C. smoke events, the classic AQHI did not reflect these increases as strongly because it combines three pollutants. The B.C. monitoring review reports that the modified model was first piloted before the 2018 wildfire season and was subsequently adopted permanently on a year-round basis. Therefore, the B.C. approach is not limited to wildfire season. [^5]

The B.C. Centre for Disease Control's May 2024 fact sheet explains that the provincial AQHI is calculated every hour in two ways: using PM<sub>2.5</sub>, NO<sub>2</sub> and O<sub>3</sub> together, and using PM<sub>2.5</sub> alone. The higher value is reported. [^1]

Research comparing AQHI amendments for B.C. wildfire seasons found that the 1-hour PM<sub>2.5</sub>-only amendment was a stronger indicator of asthma-related health outcomes than the classic AQHI in the periods studied. This finding supports the use of the additional PM<sub>2.5</sub>-based calculation; it does not mean that the modified index performed better for every health outcome. [^8]

### Methods

B.C. calculates two values:

1. **Classic AQHI:** calculated using 3-hour rolling averages of PM<sub>2.5</sub>, O<sub>3</sub> and NO<sub>2</sub>, as described in the National AQHI section.
2. **PM<sub>2.5</sub>-only AQHI:** calculated using the 1-hour average PM<sub>2.5</sub> concentration. [^5]

The PM<sub>2.5</sub>-only calculation uses the ceiling function, which rounds a value up to the next whole number:

$$
AQHI_{PM_{2.5}} =
\left\lceil
\frac{PM_{2.5,1h}}{10}
\right\rceil
$$

Where:

- \(PM_{2.5,1h}\) is the 1-hour average PM<sub>2.5</sub> concentration in µg/m³.
- \(\lceil x \rceil\) is the ceiling function: it returns the smallest integer greater than or equal to \(x\). For example, \(\lceil 4.5 \rceil = 5\). [^5]

The reported B.C. AQHI-Plus is the higher of the classic AQHI and the PM<sub>2.5</sub>-only value:

$$
AQHI_{\text{Plus}} =
\max\left(
AQHI_{\text{classic}},
AQHI_{PM_{2.5}}
\right)
$$

For example, if the 1-hour average PM<sub>2.5</sub> concentration is 45 µg/m³, the PM<sub>2.5</sub>-only value is \(\lceil 45/10 \rceil = 5\). If the classic AQHI for that hour is 3, the reported AQHI-Plus is 5. [^5]

### B.C. wildfire-smoke PM<sub>2.5</sub> categories

The BCCDC fact sheet and the B.C. Health and Smoke Exposure (HASE) Coordination Committee guideline publish the following provincial AQHI categories for wildfire-smoke communications. The values are based on 1-hour PM<sub>2.5</sub> concentrations. [^1][^2]

| 1-hour PM<sub>2.5</sub> (µg/m³) | Provincial AQHI | Risk category |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0–10 | 1 | Low |
| 11–20 | 2 | Low |
| 21–30 | 3 | Low |
| 31–40 | 4 | Moderate |
| 41–50 | 5 | Moderate |
| 51–60 | 6 | Moderate |
| 61–70 | 7 | High |
| 71–80 | 8 | High |
| 81–90 | 9 | High |
| 91–100 | 10 | High |
| 101+ | 10+ | Very High |

_Note_: Adapted from "Wildfire Smoke and the Air Quality Health Index (AQHI)" (May 2024), bccdc.ca/resource-gallery/Documents/Guidelines%20and%20Forms/Guidelines%20and%20Manuals/Health-Environment/BCCDC_WildFire_FactSheet_SmokeAndTheAQHI.pdf [^1], and "BC Health and Smoke Exposure (HASE) Coordination Committee Guideline" (July 2025), bccdc.ca/resource-gallery/Documents/Guidelines%20and%20Forms/Guidelines%20and%20Manuals/Health-Environment/BC%20Health%20Wildfire%20Smoke%20Response%20Coordination%20Guideline.pdf [^2]. Accessed 8 Nov. 2026.

The published table assigns an AQHI value of 1 to the 0–10 µg/m³ band. The B.C. monitoring review presents the ceiling formula, which mathematically returns 0 when the PM<sub>2.5</sub> concentration is exactly zero, but does not explain this boundary case. The table above therefore reproduces the published reporting categories rather than adding an undocumented adjustment to the formula. [^2][^5]

## Additional B.C. application: Kitimat SO<sub>2</sub> pilot

B.C. also tests a separate AQHI-Plus application for sulphur dioxide (SO<sub>2</sub>) in Kitimat. This is a local pilot and is distinct from the province-wide PM<sub>2.5</sub>-based AQHI-Plus method. Under the Kitimat approach, the reported value is the highest of the classic AQHI, the PM<sub>2.5</sub>-based AQHI-Plus and the SO<sub>2</sub>-based AQHI-Plus. The SO<sub>2</sub> calculation uses the highest 1-hour SO<sub>2</sub> concentration measured at the Whitesail, Riverlodge and Haisla Village community monitoring sites. [^6]

| Classic AQHI | 1-hour SO<sub>2</sub> (ppb) | SO<sub>2</sub>-based AQHI-Plus | Reported AQHI | Additional note |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1–10+ | 0–35 | N/A | Classic AQHI | None |
| 1–3 | 36–184 | N/A | Classic AQHI | Additional advice for people with chronic respiratory conditions, such as asthma |
| 4–10+ | 36–184 | N/A | Classic AQHI | None |
| 1–6 | 185+ | 7 | 7 | AQHI raised to 7 |
| 7–10+ | 185+ | 7 | Classic AQHI | Existing value retained |

_Note_: Adapted from "Kitimat SO2 Alert Pilot Project" (30 May 2023), www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/air/air-quality/measuring/kitimat-so2-alert-pilot-project [^6]. Accessed 8 Nov. 2026.

The SO<sub>2</sub>-based value is conditional. When the 1-hour SO<sub>2</sub> concentration is 185 ppb or higher, the reported AQHI is raised to 7 only when the classic AQHI is between 1 and 6. If the classic AQHI is already 7 or higher, that existing value is retained. At 36–184 ppb, an additional health message is shown for people with chronic respiratory conditions when the classic AQHI is 1–3; the index itself is not raised. [^6]
